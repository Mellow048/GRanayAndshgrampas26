pelican-rain-courier.html — one file, no dependencies, nothing pre-baked and no keyframes doing the work that physics should do. The speed you see is integrated from crank torque, quadratic aero drag, rolling resistance, the analytic road grade and a brake force capped by mu·m·g·cosθ on wet asphalt and again by g·b/h so the bird cannot endo. Wheel spin is integrated separately from ground speed, and the difference between them is the slip ratio you can watch appear when it brakes hard on the cobbles. The fork is a base-excited damped oscillator, so it rings down after the manhole dip instead of snapping back. Legs and wings are three-joint chains solved by cyclic coordinate descent with the foot angle blended in at the ankle; the saddle height is re-capped every frame by what the leg can actually reach, which is where the standing-pedal bob comes from. Readouts above are measured from the solver, not decorative.
